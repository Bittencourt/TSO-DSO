The aggregator as an actor: retail wedge and behavioural scenarios

Phase 44 (ACTOR-01..04) lets a group of site aggregators carry its own objective, lets several actors sit at one bus, and lets a population respond only partially (or not at all) to the price signal. The measurement is produced by scripts/actor_behaviour_sweep.jl and committed to results/actor_behaviour/actor_behaviour_sweep.csv. This page reads that committed artifact and recomputes every derived figure live, so no result number below is typed by hand.

1. The actor objective and the wedge

A Portfolio groups single-bus site aggregators under one objective. With WelfareObjective a site maximises its utility minus its payment at the distribution price (the default, bit-identical to the pre-phase code). With ProfitObjective(m) the actor adds a retail margin m[t] per unit of net consumption: the customers of the portfolio face DADP + m and the actor books

\[\text{profit}_k = \sum_{t} m_t \,\ell_{k,t}, \qquad \ell_{k,t} = \text{net load of the portfolio's sites}.\]

The wedge is a symmetric price shift (assumption A6). m[t] is added to the aggregator's price for net injection of EITHER sign: a net buyer pays DADP + m per unit bought, and a net seller also receives DADP + m per unit sold. It is not the usual buy-only retail margin. The actor's profit m * (net load) is therefore negative whenever its portfolio is a net exporter: the margin is then a subsidy paid on exports. The IEEE-13 ground fixture is PV-heavy and its portfolio is a net exporter, so the reported actor profit is a LOSS on every m > 0 row (the live check after the table below prints the committed numbers). For the same reason actor_objective = social welfare - profit is higher than the social welfare on those rows.

First-welfare-theorem framing (read this before interpreting any number). The wedge is a weighted objective inside the SAME price-taking equilibrium: the actor takes the DSO price as given, has no market power and does not play a Stackelberg game against the DSO price. It is not a strategic aggregator (that is deferred as ACTOR-F1). Consequently a zero wedge is a null result by design (the identity row below), and a nonzero wedge can only lower social welfare: W_soc <= W_opt, so the efficiency loss dW = W_opt - W_soc is non-negative up to the measured ADMM slack. A materially negative value would be a bug and is recorded as the verdict refused:impossible_dW, never hidden.

using TSODSO

3. Portfolios and slots (several actors at one bus)

Under ADMM the consensus variable is keyed by (actor, bus), a slot, not by the bus alone. Several sites at one bus therefore iterate separately, the published DADP is per bus, and every slot at a bus must carry the same nodal price. That agreement is reported as the slot-lambda certificate (slot_lambda_gap = max over slots of the distance to the primary slot's multiplier at the same bus, slot_lambda_agree = gap within the measured 1e-12 floor; observed gap 2.7e-15), never thrown. Portfolios are a grouping only: a portfolio spanning several buses has no shared-resource coupling between its sites (a joint capacity limit across buses is deferred as ACTOR-F2). The settlement adapters sum nets and utilities per bus on shared buses, and the actor profit is a separate, exactly conserving mirror pair (:actor, id) / (:aggregator, bus) in the Phase-41 ledger, so ledger_residual == 0.0 holds on every row of the artifact (checked live below).

    @printf("ledger_residual == 0.0 on %d of %d ok rows\n", count(r -> num(r["ledger_residual"]) == 0.0, ok), length(ok))
end

4. Behavioural scenarios (IEEE-123)

Behaviour is exogenous and seeded, built by apply_behaviour(aggs, BehaviourSpec(...); T):

  • Partial compliance xi. A wrapped device dispatches p' = b + xi (p - b) with the blended utility (1 - xi) Ub + xi U(p). This is the exact expectation over a population in which a share xi responds and the rest keeps its baseline; it is not a single household's behaviour. xi = 0 is the pure opt-out.
  • Opt-out. A seeded per-device draw (the granularity is the device, not the house) removes a share of devices from the response entirely. The opt-out is exogenous; endogenous opt-out is deferred as ACTOR-F3.
  • Rebound r. A device that curtailed must recover at least r times the curtailed energy within a window W of hours.

Baseline, stated honestly. The no-response baseline b is the FIT flat price applied per device by a single-device solve. It is not fit_baseline's output (that function returns per-aggregator flows, drops batteries, rejects EV/HeatPump/SmartInverterPV and runs a nested power flow). The rebound metric below compares the sum of these baselines against the realised net load.

The anchor row (xi = 1, opt_out = 0, no rebound) is the unwrapped population and defines W_ref. A behavioural dW is W_ref - W_row; because a wrapped device has a blended utility the first-welfare-theorem sign guarantee does NOT apply, and the sign is reported as measured.

The next figures are derived from the table. spread is the largest welfare change relative to the anchor, expressed against |W_ref|.

Null result at this fixture scale. The devices of this fixture are small against the network, so every behaviour point lands within solver noise of the anchor: the welfare spread over the whole grid is a few 1e-9 relative (3.4e-9 in the committed artifact), the mean DADP moves by under 1e-5 (7.9e-6), and the DSO price maximum is 8.9999 on every row. The signs of dW are MIXED, and some rows have a negative dW (a welfare GAIN over the unwrapped population, e.g. when a rebound constraint is added), which is impossible for a true added constraint. These tiny signed differences are therefore ADMM slack, not behaviour effects, and the sweep shows no resolvable ACTOR-04 effect on this fixture. Resolving one would need devices scaled to the feeder.

Noise reference. The repeated xi = 0 rows are bitwise identical because the solve is deterministic (everything is opted out, so the same population is solved again): their spread is exactly zero and says nothing about solver noise. The honest noise floor is the size of the sign-mixed dW of the non-trivial rows, printed as noise floor below.

5. Settlement and the rebound metric

Ledger totals per actor come from build_settlement_ledger(...; actor_wedges). On the wedge block the actor profit is the ledger_total_actor_* column; the portfolio left on welfare has no wedge row (recorded NaN). The rebound metric (rebound_amount, rebound_ratio) is computed from the summed per-device baseline against the realised net load; the ratio is NaN exactly when there is no reduction hour.

6. Refusals

Certificate refusals, solve failures and an impossible dW are rows with verdict = refused:<reason>, never dropped and never repaired by loosening a tolerance.

let bad = filter(r -> r["verdict"] != "ok", rows)
    println(isempty(bad) ? "no refused points in this artifact" : "refused points:")
    for r in bad
        println("  ", r["fixture"], " wedge=", r["wedge"], " xi=", r["xi"], " -> ", r["verdict"])
    end
end

Reproduce

julia --project=. scripts/actor_behaviour_sweep.jl regenerates the CSV (the total runtime is printed at the top of this page, recomputed from the artifact). The columns are written in a fixed order; every column except runtime_s is deterministic, while runtime_s is a wall-clock measurement and differs on every run and machine. The slow test test/test_actor_sweep.jl checks the schema and re-solves one reference wedge point.
