## The demonstration that fell out of it

Running the script's own tolerance ladder on **both** substrates produced the discriminator working
in both directions on one page — which I had not planned:

| substrate | ratio @ `1e-8` | ratio @ `1e-10` | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-bus, known-inexact control | 9608.6 | **9608.6** (identical, `maxgap` 10.44 → 10.44) | **structural** — persists |
| IEEE-123, worst point | 4.7604 | **0.0028505** (1670×) | **numerical** — collapses |
| IEEE-123, lowest-PV point | 4.0931 | **0.0030026** (1363×) | **numerical** |

Objectives agree to 7 significant figures across every tightening, so the optimum never moves.

## Correction to a spike-002 claim, found while doing this

Spike 002 Finding 5 said *"tightening tolerance is not a free fix — only 1 of 3 points reached
`1e-10`, the others return `ALMOST_OPTIMAL`."* **That was an artifact of also tightening
`tol_feas`.** This script's ladder tightens only `tol_gap_abs`/`tol_gap_rel`, and both discriminated
IEEE-123 points converge and collapse. So the discriminated count went 1/3 → 2/2, and the caveat is
narrower than recorded: tighten the *gap* tolerances, leave feasibility alone. Corrected in the page's
warning admonition, which names the earlier mistake explicitly.

The page also surfaces a free tell the findings artifact now computes automatically: the exact/inexact
band is `0.7356 … 1.088` on IEEE-123 (**1.5×**, straddling the threshold continuously) versus
`0.024 … 8.448` on the 3-bus fixture (**350×**, nothing inside). Narrow band ⇒ the boundary's position
is decided by the threshold; wide band ⇒ it isn't.

## Content the page carries

Beyond the two maps and figures: the free detector and what it costs; the control discipline (with the
inert-fixture false start named as *why*); the boundary table; the noise-floor caveat with mechanism
and the practical per-feeder calibration rule; and a closing section on **what does not generalize** —
spike 001's zero-false-negatives and cliff findings both died on the second substrate, and saying so
is the point of publishing two substrates rather than one.

The IEEE-123 result is stated as *"no **structural** inexactness was found in the swept region"* —
never "the relaxation is exact here", which would need the AC oracle or reliably converged tight
solves.

## Two dependency choices, both avoiding a pinned-manifest change

`docs/Project.toml` deliberately pins a minimal set (CairoMakie, Documenter, Literate, TSODSO), and CI
requires `docs/Manifest.toml` to stay in Julia-1.12.5 lockstep. So:

- **CSV/DataFrames:** not added — an ~8-line `Base` parser reads the committed numeric table instead.
- **JuMP:** reached via `using TSODSO.JuMP` (JuMP is already loaded as a TSODSO dependency), not by
  adding it. First draft used `using JuMP` and failed in the docs env; this is the fix.

Both choices are commented in-place with the reason.
